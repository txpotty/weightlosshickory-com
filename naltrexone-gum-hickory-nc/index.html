    How Naltrexone Gum Works for Patients in Hickory, NC

    How Naltrexone Gum Works in Hickory

    Naltrexone blocks opioid receptors, and the 50 mg tablet has been FDA-approved for alcohol and opioid use disorder for decades. A compounding pharmacy mixes a set amount of naltrexone into each piece of gum. Some of the drug may be absorbed through the lining of the mouth while you chew, and the rest is swallowed with saliva. That is the idea. Nobody has shown the gum works better than a tablet.

    Who Naltrexone Gum Is For

    Adults who would be reasonable candidates for naltrexone but dislike swallowing pills are the main group. Some ask about it as a low dose wellness option. Others are working on cravings and want something they can chew right before a known trigger, like a Friday evening or a long drive home. Opioid users are not candidates. Significant liver disease usually points to a different plan.

    Dosing and Use in Hickory

    Your provider sets the strength of each piece and how often to use it. A typical instruction is to chew slowly for roughly 20 to 30 minutes and then discard the piece rather than swallowing it. One piece a day is common. Other plans use it as needed, about an hour before a situation where cravings tend to appear, with a daily maximum written on your label. Compounded strengths vary, so follow the label exactly.

    What to Expect in the First Weeks

    The first week is mostly about learning the routine. Some people get mild nausea or a headache when they start any form of naltrexone, and a few notice lighter sleep for several nights. These usually ease within two weeks. The taste takes some getting used to. Around day 14, expect questions about how many pieces you chewed, at what times, and whether your mouth feels irritated.

    Side Effects of Naltrexone Gum in Hickory

    The side effects match other forms of naltrexone and include nausea, headache, dizziness and trouble sleeping. Chewing brings a couple of extras, such as a bitter aftertaste and sore gums. Opioids are the serious concern. Naltrexone can stop opioid pain medicine from working and can trigger withdrawal in anyone who uses opioids, which is why the screening is so specific. Yellow eyes, dark urine or bad upper belly pain need a same-day message.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your questionnaire asks about current medications, opioid use, liver health, pregnancy and breastfeeding, plus any major medical conditions. Tramadol and codeine cough syrup are asked about by name. A recent liver panel showing ALT and AST may be requested when your history raises a flag. Jaw trouble matters too. People with jaw pain or recent dental work are often pointed to capsules instead.

    How Naltrexone Gum Compares in Hickory

    Standard naltrexone tablets have decades of research and FDA approval for alcohol and opioid use disorder. Low dose naltrexone capsules are compounded and used off-label at 1.5 to 4.5 mg. Gum is mainly a preference format, newer and much less studied, picked by people who find pills hard to take. Weight loss is a different story. For medical weight loss, GLP-1 drugs such as semaglutide have far stronger trial evidence than any naltrexone format.
