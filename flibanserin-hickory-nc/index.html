    How Flibanserin Treatment Works for Women in Hickory, NC

    How Flibanserin Works in Hickory

    Flibanserin acts on serotonin receptors in the brain, not on blood flow and not on hormones. The working theory is that it rebalances serotonin, dopamine and norepinephrine, the brain chemicals tied to sexual interest. It is not a hormone. It is also nothing like Viagra, which changes blood flow and does little for desire itself. Effects build slowly over weeks of nightly use.

    Who Flibanserin Is Meant For

    The FDA approval covers premenopausal women with acquired, generalized HSDD. That means interest was fine before, it has faded across the board, and the change is causing you distress. Not every dip qualifies. Low desire driven mainly by depression, a medication such as an SSRI, a medical illness or a relationship problem is a different situation. Women past menopause fall outside the approved group.

    Dosing and the Nightly Schedule in Hickory

    The dose is one 100 mg tablet, once a day, at bedtime. Always at bedtime. Dizziness and sleepiness peak in the hours right after a dose, so taking it before sleep means most of that happens while you are already asleep. This is a daily medication, not something taken before sex. Missed a dose? Skip it, and do not double up the next night.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    Week one is mostly about side effects, usually mild sleepiness or dizziness that eases. That part usually passes. By weeks four to six, women who respond often notice they think about sex more and mind the low desire less. In the clinical trials, the average gain over placebo was modest, about one additional satisfying sexual event a month, along with less distress. Your provider checks in near week eight, and if nothing meaningful has changed, flibanserin is stopped.

    Side Effects of Flibanserin in Hickory

    The most common side effects are dizziness, sleepiness, nausea and fatigue. Dry mouth and trouble sleeping show up too. The boxed warning is about severe low blood pressure and fainting, mostly tied to alcohol and to interacting medications. Do not drive or run machinery for at least six hours after a dose. Message your provider the same day if you get lightheaded or unusually drowsy.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Screening starts with your full medication list. The big exclusion is any moderate or strong CYP3A4 inhibitor, a group that covers certain antifungals, antibiotics, HIV drugs and grapefruit juice in quantity. Liver impairment of any degree rules it out. Your provider also asks about pregnancy, breastfeeding, mood, fainting history and how much you drink in a normal week.

    Flibanserin Compared With PT-141 and Other Options in Hickory

    Flibanserin and bremelanotide, sold as Vyleesi and often called PT-141, are the two FDA-approved medicines for HSDD before menopause. The difference is mostly routine: flibanserin is a nightly tablet, while PT-141 is a small injection used about 45 minutes before sex. Women who drink regularly sometimes lean toward PT-141 because the alcohol rules are simpler. Compounded options such as oxytocin are not FDA-approved for this use, and your provider will say so. Your provider explains which fits.
